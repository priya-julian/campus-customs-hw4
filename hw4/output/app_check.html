Campus Customs — App Check

Three checks against the running site. Every figure quoted below was also run against campus_customs.db directly, and matched.

Check 1 — The chat reports real stock and price from the database

On the Boola Boola T Shirt page, Handsome Dan was asked: “How many of these do you have left in medium, and what does it cost?”

What this proves. Dan answered “The Boola Boola T Shirt has 15 left in medium. It costs $32” — the same numbers the product page shows in its size tiles, because both read the same inventory and catalogue rows. The tiles also show L sold out, which is the honest-stock behaviour the shop is built around.

Check 2 — A category question puts matching products on the page

Starting on the About Us page, Dan was asked “What hoodies do you have?”

What this proves. The agent returned structured product matches and the front end rendered them as cards — a “From your chat” band reading 6 matches for “What hoodies do you have?”, with image, name, price and short description, above the full 102-item catalogue. Each card is the same component as the catalogue grid, so clicking one opens the single-item page built in Problem 3.

Check 3 — Usability: size availability on cards, and a “my size” filter

One of the Problem 9 improvements. Every card carries a strip of six size pips, with unavailable sizes greyed and struck through; the toolbar can narrow the grid to a single size.

What this proves. A shopper can see which sizes are on the shelf without opening a single product page, and can cut the grid to what they can actually buy. The grid renders 612 pips — one per inventory row — with exactly 145 struck through, the number of zero-quantity rows in the table. Filtering to XS gives 75 items, which is exactly what SQL returns.
